# Temperament And Tuning Reference Bible

This is a working reference for the Interval Temperament Visualizer. It focuses on the concepts, ratios, cents, and listening/visual consequences that matter when comparing equal temperament, just intonation, Pythagorean tuning, and related historical temperaments.

## Fast Definitions

| Term | Practical meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Frequency ratio | The multiplier between two pitches. A 3/2 fifth means the upper tone vibrates 1.5 times as fast as the lower tone. |
| Octave | Ratio 2/1. Pitches one octave apart are treated as the same pitch class in most Western tuning systems. |
| Cents | Logarithmic pitch unit. One octave is 1200 cents, one equal-tempered semitone is 100 cents. |
| Just intonation | Tuning by whole-number ratios, usually chosen from low harmonic relationships such as 3/2, 5/4, and 6/5. |
| Temperament | A deliberate compromise that adjusts pure intervals so a fixed-pitch instrument can play more notes, keys, or chords. |
| Equal temperament | The octave is divided into 12 equal logarithmic steps. Each semitone is `2^(1/12)`. |
| Pythagorean tuning | Tuning generated by stacking pure 3/2 fifths and reducing by octaves. |
| Meantone temperament | A family of temperaments that narrows fifths so thirds become smoother than in Pythagorean tuning. |
| Comma | A small mismatch between different ways of reaching nearly the same pitch. Important commas include the Pythagorean comma and syntonic comma. |
| Wolf interval | The badly mistuned leftover interval created when comma error is concentrated in one place. |
| Enharmonic pair | Two spellings treated as the same key in equal temperament, such as C# and Db. In many non-equal systems they are different pitches. |
| Root | The reference pitch from which a ratio is measured. In the visualizer, Oscillator X functions as the just-intonation root. |

## Just Intonation

Just intonation tunes intervals as whole-number ratios. The point is not that there is only one just scale; the point is that the intervals are rational frequency relationships. A practical just scale chooses a specific set of ratios for a specific root and musical purpose.

### Common 5-Limit Chromatic Ratios

| Note from C | Ratio | Cents | Interval name |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| C | 1/1 | 0.00 | Root |
| Db | 16/15 | 111.73 | Minor second |
| D | 9/8 | 203.91 | Major second |
| Eb | 6/5 | 315.64 | Minor third |
| E | 5/4 | 386.31 | Major third |
| F | 4/3 | 498.04 | Perfect fourth |
| F# | 45/32 | 590.22 | Augmented fourth |
| G | 3/2 | 701.96 | Perfect fifth |
| Ab | 8/5 | 813.69 | Minor sixth |
| A | 5/3 | 884.36 | Major sixth |
| Bb | 9/5 | 1017.60 | Minor seventh |
| B | 15/8 | 1088.27 | Major seventh |
| C | 2/1 | 1200.00 | Octave |

### Just Intonation Strengths

- Simple ratios draw simple Lissajous figures and tend to sound settled when the root is correct.
- The 5/4 major third and 6/5 minor third are smoother than their equal-tempered equivalents.
- Harmonic-series relationships make interval identity clear in drones, sustained tones, and two-oscillator comparisons.

### Just Intonation Tradeoffs

- Ratios are root-dependent. A pitch that is just against C may not be just against D, E, or A.
- Fixed-pitch keyboards cannot keep every chord pure without changing pitch assignments.
- Similar interval names can have multiple just sizes. For example, 9/8 and 10/9 are both tones, but they are not the same size.
- Enharmonic pairs are not automatically equivalent.

## Equal Temperament

12-tone equal temperament divides the octave into twelve equal logarithmic parts. It makes all semitone steps identical and collapses enharmonic pairs: C# and Db are the same key, G# and Ab are the same key, and so on.

### Equal Temperament Strengths

- Every key has the same interval pattern.
- Modulation is easy because no key owns the pure intervals.
- Fixed keyboards, fretted instruments, and digital instruments can use one pitch map across the chromatic system.
- Notation and UI controls are simpler because enharmonic spellings can map to one frequency.

### Equal Temperament Tradeoffs

- Except for unisons and octaves, equal-tempered intervals are not pure whole-number ratios.
- Major thirds are about 13.69 cents sharp compared with 5/4.
- Minor thirds are about 15.64 cents flat compared with 6/5.
- Fifths are close to pure but still about 1.96 cents flat.

## Pythagorean Tuning

Pythagorean tuning builds notes by stacking pure fifths with ratio 3/2, then moving notes by octaves until they fit into the chosen octave.

### Construction Pattern

Starting from a reference pitch:

```text
up a fifth: multiply by 3/2
down a fifth: multiply by 2/3
reduce to one octave: multiply or divide by powers of 2
```

A common D-centered chain is:

```text
Eb - Bb - F - C - G - D - A - E - B - F# - C# - G#
```

### Pythagorean Major Scale On C

| Degree | Ratio | Cents | Step to next |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| C | 1/1 | 0.00 | 9/8 |
| D | 9/8 | 203.91 | 9/8 |
| E | 81/64 | 407.82 | 256/243 |
| F | 4/3 | 498.04 | 9/8 |
| G | 3/2 | 701.96 | 9/8 |
| A | 27/16 | 905.87 | 9/8 |
| B | 243/128 | 1109.78 | 256/243 |
| C | 2/1 | 1200.00 | - |

### Pythagorean Character

- Pure fifths and fourths are the tuning's main virtue.
- Major seconds at 9/8 are strong and clean.
- Major thirds are high and rough compared with just 5/4.
- Minor thirds are low compared with just 6/5.
- The system preserves spelling distinctions that equal temperament hides.

### Wolf Fifth

Twelve pure 3/2 fifths do not land exactly on seven octaves. In a 12-note Pythagorean keyboard, eleven fifths can remain pure, but the remaining fifth absorbs the comma error and becomes the wolf interval. Wikipedia's D-based example describes the leftover diminished sixth from G# to Eb as the wolf fifth. Changing the chain moves the wolf, but it does not remove it.

## Commas And Small Mismatches

| Comma | Ratio | Cents | What it measures |

| Quarter Pythagorean comma | `(531441/524288)^(1/4)` | 5.87 | A quarter of the Pythagorean comma. |
| Quarter syntonic comma | `(81/80)^(1/4)` | 5.38 | A quarter of the syntonic comma, central to quarter-comma meantone. |

## Quarter-Comma Meantone

Quarter-comma meantone narrows each fifth by one quarter of the syntonic comma. The goal is to make the major third land at 5/4 after four fifth steps.

### Practical Identity

| Feature | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Fifths | Slightly narrow compared with pure 3/2. |
| Major thirds | Much smoother than Pythagorean thirds; the central major third can be pure 5/4. |
| Keys | Good in nearby keys, poor where the wolf interval appears. |
| Enharmonics | C# and Db are different pitches, not one equal-tempered key. |
| Visual behavior | Frazer's Lissajous comparison shows meantone thirds as especially stable compared with equal-tempered thirds. |

## Werckmeister III

Werckmeister III is a circulating temperament: it distributes error so more keys are usable than in narrow meantone systems while keeping distinct key color. It does not make all keys identical like equal temperament.

## Harmonic Functions And Stacked Fourths/Fifths

Quartal harmony builds sonorities by stacking fourths, such as C-F-Bb. Quintal harmony builds by stacking fifths, such as C-G-D. Since fourths and fifths invert into each other, the same pitch collection can often be read either way after revoicing.

### Why This Matters For A Tuning Visualizer

- Quartal voicings foreground fourth and fifth relationships, which are nearly pure in equal temperament and exactly pure in many just/Pythagorean contexts.
- Stacked fifths are the engine of Pythagorean tuning.
- Fourth/fifth stacks sound less root-directed than tertian triads because their interval cycle is more symmetrical.
- The same notes can imply different roots depending on voicing, register, and melodic context.

## Listening Guide

| What to compare | What to listen for |
| --- | --- |
| Equal E over C vs just E over C | Equal major third beats more; just 5/4 settles more strongly. |
| Pythagorean E over C vs just E over C | Pythagorean 81/64 is sharper and less triad-friendly. |
| Equal G over C vs just/Pythagorean G over C | The fifth difference is subtle because equal fifths are only about 1.96 cents flat. |
| Just Bb over C | The 9/5 minor seventh is wider than equal and can feel expressive rather than dominant-function standard. |
| Pythagorean C# vs Db | They should not be treated as identical when spelling is part of the system. |
| Meantone E over C | The third is the selling point; the fifth is the compromise. |

## Visual / Lissajous Guide

The visualizer's XY trace is directly relevant to tuning:

- Simple whole-number ratios produce repeating closed shapes.
- Lower-number ratios usually make simpler, more stable figures.
- A pure octave 2/1, fifth 3/2, fourth 4/3, and major third 5/4 each has a distinctive closed figure.
- Equal-tempered intervals that are irrational ratios do not close perfectly in the same way; the trace drifts or slowly moves instead.
- Tempered intervals close only approximately unless the visualization uses a rational approximation.
- The visual difference between equal and just thirds is usually easier to see than the difference between equal and just fifths.

## System Comparison

| System | Generator / principle | Best at | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Just intonation | Whole-number ratios from a chosen root | Drones, pure dyads, smooth triads, harmonic-series pedagogy | Root-dependent; hard to modulate on fixed keys. |
| Pythagorean tuning | Pure 3/2 fifths reduced by octaves | Melodic lines, fourths, fifths, medieval/ancient theory | Harsh thirds; wolf interval in 12-note layouts. |
| Quarter-comma meantone | Narrow fifths to purify major thirds | Smooth common triads in favored keys | Enharmonic splits and wolf intervals. |
| Werckmeister III | Distributed comma compromise | More keys than meantone with key character retained | Not uniformly transposable like equal temperament. |
| 12-TET | Equal logarithmic semitones | Modulation, fixed keyboards, modern notation, simple UI | Most intervals are not pure ratios. |

## Enharmonic Spelling Rules

| Equal-tempered habit | Non-equal reality |
| --- | --- |
| C# = Db | Often false. The two spellings may come from different chains or functions. |
| G# = Ab | Often false. Pythagorean examples place them about a comma apart. |
| One semitone size | Often false. Pythagorean tuning has diatonic and chromatic semitones of different sizes. |
| All keys equivalent | False outside equal temperament. Each key can have different interval quality. |
| Ratio belongs to the key name | Usually false. The ratio belongs to a pitch relative to a chosen root. |

## Practical Design Notes For This App

- Keep equal temperament as the stable default reference because it matches common keyboard expectations.
- Keep just intonation root-bound. Changing Oscillator X should recalculate just Y from the selected interval ratio.
- When adding Pythagorean behavior, preserve enharmonic spelling; C# and Db are not the same theoretical note in that system.
- When showing cents deltas, state the comparison target clearly: equal-vs-just, equal-vs-Pythagorean, or Pythagorean-vs-just.
- For visual education, prioritize intervals where differences are visible and audible: major third, minor third, tritone, sixths, sevenths.
- For fourths and fifths, emphasize subtle beating and trace drift rather than dramatic visual contrast.

## Glossary

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Diatonic | Belonging to the main seven-note scale structure. |
| Chromatic | Involving altered notes or semitone motion outside the basic diatonic pattern. |
| Tertian | Harmony built from thirds, such as ordinary major and minor triads. |
| Quartal | Harmony built from fourths. |
| Quintal | Harmony built from fifths. |
| Ratiometric | Based on explicit ratios rather than equal logarithmic division. |
| Tempered fifth | A fifth adjusted away from 3/2. |
| Pure interval | An interval tuned to a simple whole-number ratio. |
| Beating | Audible amplitude pulsing caused by close but mismatched frequencies. |
| Drift | Visual or phase movement caused by an interval that is close to, but not exactly, a simple ratio. |

## Minimum Mental Model

Equal temperament is convenient because it makes every semitone the same size. Just intonation is compelling because simple ratios sound and draw cleanly against the right root. Pythagorean tuning is built from beautiful fifths but pays for them with rough thirds and a comma problem. Meantone shifts the compromise toward smooth thirds. Circulating temperaments distribute the compromise so more keys work while keeping different key colors. Every tuning system answers the same question differently: which intervals should be pure, and where should the error go?
